Learning from Plants

A visual exploration of cactus forms, perceived aggression, and the distance between appearance and interpretation.

Role

Art & design practice

Context

Plant morphology / perception / visual storytelling

Appearance and interpretation

Beginning with the diversity of leaf edges, this project asks how familiar visual cues lead us to judge plants. A cactus becomes the focus for examining perceived aggression, adaptation, and the assumptions viewers bring to natural forms.

Changing the scale of observation

The narrative unfolds through three approaches: possessing, changing, and producing a sense of aggression. Moving between close and distant views, the work uses visual contrast and humor to reconsider how partial observations shape interpretation.

Beginning with leaf-edge morphology, I explore whether the edges of leaves appear aggressive. Our visual judgments are shaped by familiar experience, while plant forms reflect adaptation to environmental and genetic conditions. Just as partial views of social events can produce cognitive bias, looking at plants invites a broader frame of observation—from the microscopic to the macroscopic, and from isolated forms to their environments.
