Interactive installation · Bio-art design

Visible Breathing

A plant-like form changes its movement in response to the presence of a viewer.

Role

Art & design practice

Context

Proximity / movement / human–plant relationships

This work stages an environmental experiment between people and a plant-like form. By reshaping viewers’ visual and emotional perceptions, it invites reflection on human–nature relationships.

The “plant” changes with the viewer’s distance: when no one is nearby, it appears to breathe; when someone approaches, it becomes still—as if nature were choosing whether to reveal its state in the presence of humans. The encounter unfolds over time.

Moving between subject–object, subject–subject, and object–object relationships, the work changes the scale of observation. Participants experience a situation in which nature appears to choose how it is perceived, opening another way to locate themselves within the world.

An encounter shaped by distance

The installation concept presents a plant-like form that moves as if breathing when no one is nearby, then becomes still when a viewer approaches. This reversal invites the viewer to consider what it means for nature to reveal or withhold itself.

Repositioning the viewer

Approaching and stepping away become part of the work. The encounter examines relationships between subject and object, using movement and stillness to encourage reflection on how humans position themselves within the natural world.
